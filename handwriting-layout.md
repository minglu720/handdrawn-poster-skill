# Handwriting Layout

## Purpose

Control how handwritten letters, lines and adjacent artwork
relate on the page.

Use this file with type 3 in typography.md.
The approved third-version poster supplies the base lettering character:
fine, light, open and relaxed.

These rules add occasional deviations and natural spacing variation.
They do not replace that base with exaggerated crooked lettering.

## Scope

Apply to image-guided or individually drawn handwriting.

Types 4 and 5 retain normal font construction and readable typesetting
unless the user explicitly requests expressive placement.

Do not distort a real font letter by letter merely to simulate handwriting.

## 1. Occasional letter accents

Allow a few letters to depart more noticeably from their neighbors:

- Lean farther left or right.
- Become slightly taller or wider.
- Sit a little higher or lower.
- Use an unusually open or compressed shape.

Keep most letters closer to the relaxed base style.
An accent should feel like an incidental writing gesture.

Do not give every letter a conspicuous rotation.
Do not alternate left and right tilt in a repeating pattern.
Do not use identical distortion amounts across a word.

For short titles, one or two distinctive deviations may be enough.
For longer copy, distribute accents sparsely.

Protect recognition of numbers, punctuation and exact identifiers.

## 2. Uneven line edges

A handwritten line does not need perfectly aligned tops and bottoms.

Allow local variation in:

- Letter height.
- Baseline position.
- Ascender and descender length.
- Vertical placement of neighboring letters.

The line should retain a readable overall direction,
while individual letters drift above or below it.

Avoid a mathematically straight baseline with identical letter heights.
Also avoid a regular wave, staircase or alternating high-low pattern.

The upper and lower edges should emerge from individual letterforms,
not from a decorative distortion applied to the whole line.

Keep word boundaries and reading order clear.

## 3. Text near an irregular drawing

When text sits near a slanted, curved or uneven drawn line,
allow the gap between text and drawing to vary locally.

Do not automatically straighten the drawing,
flatten the text baseline or force a uniform parallel gap.

Text may loosely respond to a neighboring contour,
but it does not need to follow every bend.

Preserve a visible buffer:
letters should not accidentally collide with or merge into the drawing.

If the user approves an existing illustration and requests typography changes,
preserve that illustration's shape, placement and scale.

## 4. Natural variation between lines

Allow neighboring handwritten lines to have slightly different:

- Vertical positions.
- Gentle overall inclinations.
- Starting and ending positions.
- Local distances between their visible letter edges.

Letter height, baseline drift and descenders may produce uneven
local gaps between adjacent lines.

Do not force the next line to mirror every movement of the previous line.
Let their relationship remain loose and readable.

Keep related information visibly grouped.
Maintain a larger separation between distinct information blocks
than within a single block.

Avoid accidental overlaps, crowded descenders and ambiguous reading order.

## 5. Control by text role

### Title

Allow the clearest individual letter accents.
Preserve the title's overall word shape and scale controls.

### Short description

Use quieter letter variation and a gently wandering baseline.
Keep the sentence readable as a continuous thought.

### Date, time and location

Allow light irregularity while protecting exact information.
Numbers, colons, dashes, punctuation and handles must remain unambiguous.

### Longer passages

Reduce individual deviations and stabilize the overall line direction.
Use an enabled supporting font if handwriting cannot remain readable
at the intended output size.

## 6. Composition

Use irregularity within a coherent composition.

Stable margins, clear information grouping and intentional open space
can support lively handwritten placement.

Do not make every block crooked.
Do not fill open space merely because the lettering is irregular.
Do not require centered or asymmetric placement in every poster;
choose alignment according to the content.

## Prompt guidance

Describe the desired relationships explicitly:

"Fine, light handwriting with gently varied letter heights and spacing.
A few letters lean more noticeably or sit slightly above or below
their neighbors. The line edges are naturally uneven, without a
repeating pattern. Gaps between handwritten lines and nearby drawing
vary locally while remaining open and readable."

Avoid relying only on instructions such as:

- Make it random.
- Make it messy.
- Make every letter crooked.
- Increase distortion.
- Rotate the whole line.

These can produce exaggerated or mechanical effects.

## Quality gate

Check that:

- The fine, relaxed lettering base remains visible.
- Stronger letter deviations are occasional.
- Line edges vary without a repetitive pattern.
- Local spacing responds naturally to letterforms and adjacent artwork.
- Information grouping and reading order remain clear.
- Text does not accidentally collide with artwork or neighboring lines.
- Practical information remains exact and readable.

If the result feels too tidy, adjust a few letters and local gaps first.
If it feels forced or chaotic, reduce the number and magnitude
of deviations rather than removing all hand-drawn character.
